# Essential notions

In [a first notebook](sys_lin_sympy.ipynb), the use of the SymPy module and of exact computation in the field of rationals makes it possible to point out the limits of the tools available in the libraries (SciPy), including for computing the conditioning of the Hilbert matrix, a limit which is then analysed. For the Laplacian matrix, however, everything seems more reasonable for relatively large matrix sizes and the library tools are efficient. This study allows a reasoned and critical use of the available tools and libraries.


In [a second notebook](pivot.ipynb) we propose a numerical demonstration, on a set of random matrices, of the importance of choosing a stable algorithm, in particular when we have to tackle more or less ill-conditioned problems, which will be the norm in most of the interesting problems in the course (in particular for solving the ODEs and PDEs treated in the last four chapters of the course). The use of high-precision computation with mpmath gives access to the "forward" error. Consequently, according to the results of the course (Chapter 1) and since the conditioning is defined analytically, we can gain access to the "backward" error for three algorithms: solving by Gaussian elimination without pivoting, with partial pivoting and with total pivoting. Combining the knowledge of the stability of these three algorithms and of their algorithmic complexity makes it possible to conclude on the best choice for problems in practice.
