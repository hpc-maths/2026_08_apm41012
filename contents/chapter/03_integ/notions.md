# Essential notions


The first key question that will occupy us in what follows is whether it is better to consider a single interval and increase the order of the quadrature, as seen in theory in the course. But this first raises the question of the conditioning and of the stability of the algorithms as the order increases, a legitimate question if the reader recalls the studies proposed in Chapter 2. In the [first notebook](erreur.ipynb) we therefore put the usual techniques (Newton-Cotes, Clenshaw-Curtis, Gauss-Legendre) to the test on a single interval and for a simple function that does not generate a Runge phenomenon, as the order increases, and we study the "forward" error, the conditioning and the stability of the algorithms.

In the [second notebook](ordre.ipynb), we are going to study the efficiency and the accuracy of the methods by plotting, in the way that is classical in numerical analysis, the logarithm of the error as a function of the logarithm of the number of function evaluations, seen as an estimate of the computational cost. This makes it possible to check the order of the method in the logarithmic diagram and to compare the methods in a cost / accuracy diagram. This type of diagram, plotted for several typical functions representative of the cases encountered in practice, makes it possible to conclude which methods offer the best efficiency (lowest cost for a given accuracy, or maximum accuracy for a given cost).
