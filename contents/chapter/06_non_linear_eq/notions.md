# Essential notions

For a single nonlinear equation, there is a fairly long list of methods (regula falsi, chord method, secant method, Newton's method) and we illustrate in [a first notebook](./newton_secant.ipynb), for a well-conditioned problem, the evolution of the magnitude of the error, of the residual and of the increment, in order to understand what the stopping criterion should be (a recurring issue with this type of method) for Newton's method and for the secant method. A graphical interpretation makes it possible to understand clearly, on the one hand the method itself, and on the other hand the question of the order of convergence of the method, a point that is generally poorly assimilated by students.

In [a second notebook](./thermal_explosion.ipynb), we consider the solution of a nonlinear system in the spirit of the problems we have dealt with in connection with PDEs semi-discretised in space. This is the thermal explosion problem, extensively studied by the Russian school (Semenov - Frank-Kamenetskii in the 1920s / 1940s). We look for a steady solution of the heat equation with a nonlinear source term corresponding to the heating produced by an exothermic chemical reaction. When the Frank-Kamenetskii parameter is smaller than a critical value - the notion of limit point / bifurcation - there exists a unique regular solution to this PDE with Dirichlet boundary conditions (that is to say, the boundary fluxes manage to balance the heat production due to the chemical reactions inside). We study the impact of the conditioning of this problem on the convergence of Newton's method.
