# Introduction

Finite-dimensional continuous-time dynamical systems, or systems of ordinary differential equations, appear in a countless number of applications and [the first notebook](./examples.ipynb) offers a "guided tour" of several dynamical systems. These examples mix conservative and dissipative systems and come from population dynamics, celestial mechanics, nonlinear chemical dynamics or climate modelling.
They display a variety of dynamics: attracting critical point of the dynamics, limit cycle, periodic solution, chaotic system…


Building on the lecture, which provides a precise view of the notion of solution for a system of ordinary differential equations that is mathematically well posed in the sense of Hadamard, one of the objectives of the course is the numerical integration of the dynamics of these systems. In this course we focus on the explicit and implicit Euler schemes.
[A second notebook](./euler.ipynb) studies the robustness of these first-order solution methods in the case of a differential equation modelling thermal explosion. We show that the numerical analysis of these methods is a necessity in order to allow an efficient approximation.
