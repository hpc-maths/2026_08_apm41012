# Essential notions

Iterative methods have existed for a long time, introduced by Jacobi and Gauss (as well as Ludwig von Seidel), but some of them can converge slowly. Iterative methods have come back into fashion in order to tackle large-scale problems, in particular in the framework of high-performance computing for sparse matrices and for today's realistic problems. On the other hand, we have fewer theoretical results than for direct methods and numerical experimentation is important here.

In this chapter, we start with [an implementation of the stationary Jacobi and Gauss-Seidel methods](methodes.ipynb) on the classical case of the Poisson equation with a source term and Dirichlet boundary conditions. The convergence history is put into perspective with the one obtained by a conjugate gradient method in order to analyse the efficiency of the non-stationary method.

In [a second notebook](gradient.ipynb), the benefit of the conjugate gradient approach compared with a classical gradient method, or even an optimal-step one, is demonstrated in the very simple case of a 2x2 matrix, with a graphical illustration of the "descent" history.

It seemed essential to us to illustrate clearly the fact that solving the Poisson equation in 1D, 2D or 3D, with a fixed total number of degrees of freedom, requires different algorithms in order to be efficient. This case was suggested to us by Y. Saad! We take N=122500 degrees of freedom, which corresponds to a 2D case 350x350 or a 3D case 50x50x49. By choosing on the one hand the conjugate gradient approach as the iterative method, and on the other hand Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting, we can conclude that direct approaches are efficient in the 1D and 2D cases but become very heavy in 3D (bandwidth / cost of pivoting), whereas the iterative approach is particularly interesting in this 3D case (but very poor in the 1D case because of the condition number). Indeed, the condition number is relatively friendly in the 3D case and leads to fast convergence. The observation made on this simple case has a fairly general scope.
